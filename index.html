How picks are chosen

The same four-step screen is used for all three holding periods. Only the time window and the size of the targets change.

  1. Market backdrop first: find the sectors that are rising while the index falls.
  2. A dated catalyst inside the window: earnings, a contract, an upgrade, monthly data or a policy decision.
  3. Hard numbers behind the story: estimates, margins, order values and price levels, with sources on every pick.
  4. An exit plan before entry: each pick gets a buy zone, a sell target and a stop-loss. The target is usually further away than the stop.

Reading a pick

  • Today's price: the latest close. It refreshes automatically once the daily price update is running; until then it shows the research-date close.
  • Buy zone: the range to enter in. The label under the price tells you if it's in the zone, above it (wait) or below it (check why).
  • Sell target / Stop-loss: take profit here, or exit here if the idea is wrong. R:R is reward-to-risk measured from the middle of the buy zone.
  • Open any row to see the reasons, the numbers, what could go wrong, and the sources.

How past picks are judged

  • ✅ Met expectation: the target was hit. ❌ Wrong call: the stop was hit, or the trade ended at a loss.
  • 🟡 Partly right: the trade ended in profit but short of the target. The percentage shows how much of the expected gain it delivered.
  • Every batch is also compared with its market index (S&P 500 or Nifty 50) over the same window.

Keeping it fresh

A GitHub Action runs scripts/update_prices.py after the NSE and NYSE close each weekday. It updates prices and scores every past pick. To add new picks, put a new batch (with "horizon": "daily", "weekly" or "monthly") at the top of data/picks.js.

Honest limitations

  • Research-date prices come from news and quote pages, not an exchange feed. A few references are from a day or two earlier, and each card notes when.
  • Over a week or a month, much of a stock's move is noise. A good plan improves the odds but doesn't guarantee a profit.
