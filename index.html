Stock Picks
Not financial advice. Research ideas from public news and official closing prices. Check the live price with your broker before buying, and size trades so a stop-loss doesn't hurt much.

Scoring is conservative: if the stop and the target are touched on the same day, the stop counts. Returns are from each pick's reference close and exclude brokerage and taxes.

How picks are chosen

All three holding periods follow one rulebook (CRITERIA.md in the repo). A stock is published only if it passes every check below and has a dated reason to move. If fewer than 5 qualify, fewer are published; the list is never padded.

  1. Read the market mood. The S&P 500 and Nifty 50 are compared with their 50- and 200-day averages. In a weak ("risk-off") market, picks lean to steadier leaders and there are at most 3.
  2. Screen for strength. Liquid stocks in an uptrend that are beating their index. Research shows 3–12-month winners tend to keep winning, while stocks that just spiked tend to give some back, so the screen favours steady leaders over last week's rockets.
  3. Require a dated catalyst. Earnings that beat and raised guidance, fresh analyst upgrades, order wins, scheduled data. Buying just before unreported earnings is a coin flip: never for daily picks, at most once per weekly or monthly list, and always flagged.
  4. Size the levels to real volatility. Buy zone, stop and target use ATR (the stock's average daily range), so each pick's risk fits how much it normally moves.

Reading a pick

  • Status pill: ✓ Buy zone means the last close sits inside the buy zone; Wait means it's above (don't chase); Below zone or Below stop means check why or skip.
  • The bar: red dot = stop-loss, shaded band = buy zone, green dot = target, white dot = last close.
  • Nifty 50 strip at the top: refreshed about every 10 minutes during NSE hours (9:15 AM–3:30 PM IST) from Yahoo Finance, so it can lag the exchange by up to ~15 minutes. Outside market hours it shows the last close.
  • Prices are official closes of the last completed session, updated after each market close (about 4:30 PM IST for India, 3 AM IST for the US). They are not live: your broker shows the live price, so compare it with the buy zone before buying.

How prices are kept accurate

  • A GitHub job downloads official daily closes (Yahoo Finance) for the S&P 500 and Nifty 200 after each close and computes every average, ATR and relative-strength figure. A session is never used until its market has closed.
  • New picks are re-checked against the official close of the last completed session. If the research price was more than 0.3% off, the buy zone, target and stop are moved by the same percentage ("re-anchored"); the details are inside each pick under Price check & rule checks.
  • Sources: exchange filings, company releases, Reuters, Bloomberg, Business Standard, Economic Times, Mint, Moneycontrol, CNBC and similar, recent (≤ 3 days daily, 7 weekly, 21 monthly). No SEO blogs or social-media tips.

How results are judged

  • Each finished pick shows Suggested buy, Expected return (if the target is hit), Closed at (session, week-end or month-end close), Actual return, and Target met? (✅ if the price touched the target, with how far the close ended from it).
  • The Results tab keeps every list ever published, grouped by month; filter by date, week or month. Nothing is deleted. The hit rate feeds back into the rules: targets tighten if too few are reached, entries tighten if too many stops are hit.

Honest limitations

  • Over days or weeks much of a stock's move is noise. The rules improve the odds and cap the damage; they can't make every pick a winner. Judge them by the hit rate in Results.
  • Returns exclude brokerage, taxes and slippage.

  • Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999), Do Industries Explain Momentum?, Journal of Finance.
  • Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems (average true range).
