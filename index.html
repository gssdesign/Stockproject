How picks are chosen

All three holding periods follow one rulebook (CRITERIA.md in the repo). A stock is published only if it passes every check below and has a dated reason to move. If fewer than 5 qualify, fewer are published; the list is never padded.

  1. Read the market regime. The S&P 500 and Nifty 50 are compared with their 50- and 200-day averages. In a weak ("risk-off") market, picks lean to steadier leaders and there are fewer of them.
  2. Screen for strength. Liquid stocks in an uptrend that are beating their index (details below). Research on momentum shows 3–12-month winners tend to keep winning, while stocks that just spiked tend to give some back, so the screen favors steady leaders over last week's rockets.
  3. Require a dated catalyst. Ranked by evidence: earnings that beat and raised guidance (prices keep drifting after good news for weeks), fresh analyst upgrades, material order wins, scheduled data (monthly sales, rate decisions), and a sector tailwind. Buying just before an unreported earnings result is a coin flip: it's never used for daily picks, and at most once per weekly or monthly list, clearly flagged. Any earnings date inside a pick's window is shown on the pick.
  4. Set levels from real volatility. Buy zone, stop and target are sized with ATR (the stock's average daily range), so every pick's risk is proportionate to how much it normally moves.

How prices are kept accurate

  • Real exchange closes. A GitHub job runs after each market close. It downloads official daily prices (Yahoo Finance) for the S&P 500 and Nifty 200 and computes every average, ATR and relative-strength figure. Picks are priced from that data, not from news snippets.
  • Automatic verification. After new picks are published, the same job checks each reference price against the official close of the last completed session. ✓ verified close means it matched. ↻ re-anchored means it was more than 0.3% off, so the buy zone, target and stop were shifted by the same percentage (the original numbers are shown on the pick). ⏳ pending means the reference session hasn't closed yet.
  • Rule checks on every pick. Open a pick to see which checks it passed at the pick date. Hover over a chip for the numbers.
  • Sources: exchange filings, company releases, Reuters, Bloomberg, Business Standard, Economic Times, Mint, Moneycontrol, CNBC and similar. Sources must be recent (≤ 3 days for daily, 7 for weekly, 21 for monthly). No SEO blogs or social-media tips.

Reading a pick

  • Last close: the official closing price of the most recent completed session (date shown), updated after each market close. It is not a live price: during market hours your broker (Kite, etc.) shows the live price, which will differ. The label under it says whether that close was in the buy zone, above it (wait, don't chase) or below it (check why). Always compare the live price with the buy zone before buying.
  • Buy zone: enter only inside it. Sell target: take profit. Stop-loss: exit if wrong. R:R = reward ÷ risk from the middle of the zone.

How past picks are judged

  • Suggested buy, Expected return (if the target is reached), Closed at (the session, week-end or month-end close), Actual return (vs the reference close), and Target met? (✅ if the price touched the target at any point, with how far the close ended from it).
  • Open a row for the stop-loss and what happened if you followed the plan exactly. Each batch is compared with its index over the same window. The rolling hit rate feeds back into the next picks: targets are tightened if too few are reached, and entries are tightened if too many stops are hit.

Your history is kept permanently

Every batch ever published stays on the site with all its details and final result. Use Showing picks from on the Picks tab, or browse Past picks & results by month. Finished batches move into a monthly archive after 45 days that loads when you open it. Nothing is deleted.

Honest limitations

  • Over days or weeks, much of a stock's move is noise. The rules improve the odds and cap the damage; they can't make every pick a winner. Watch the hit rate in Past picks & results.
  • Returns shown exclude brokerage, taxes and slippage. Picks published before any automatic price check existed (30 Sep 2026) were researched from news prices and are marked by their verification badge.

Research behind the rules

  • Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers, Journal of Finance: medium-term momentum.
  • Jegadeesh (1990), Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns, Journal of Finance: short-term reversal.
  • Bernard & Thomas (1989), Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift, Journal of Accounting Research.
  • Womack (1996), Do Brokerage Analysts' Recommendations Have Investment Value?, Journal of Finance.
  • Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999), Do Industries Explain Momentum?, Journal of Finance.
  • Wilder (1978), New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems: average true range (ATR).
