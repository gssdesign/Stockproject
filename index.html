How picks are chosen

The same four-step screen is used for all three holding periods. Only the time window and the size of the targets change.

  1. Market backdrop first: find the sectors that are rising while the index falls.
  2. A dated catalyst inside the window: earnings, a contract, an upgrade, monthly data or a policy decision.
  3. Hard numbers behind the story: estimates, margins, order values and price levels, with sources on every pick.
  4. An exit plan before entry: each pick gets a buy zone, a sell target and a stop-loss. The target is usually further away than the stop.

Reading a pick

  • Today's price: the latest close. It refreshes automatically once the daily price update is running; until then it shows the research-date close.
  • Buy zone: the range to enter in. The label under the price tells you if it's in the zone, above it (wait) or below it (check why).
  • Sell target / Stop-loss: take profit here, or exit here if the idea is wrong. R:R is reward-to-risk measured from the middle of the buy zone.
  • Open any row to see the reasons, the numbers, what could go wrong, and the sources.

How past picks are judged

  • Suggested buy: the buy zone given on the day of the pick, plus the reference close that all returns are measured from.
  • Expected return: the gain if the sell target is reached.
  • Closed at: the closing price at the end of the holding period. For daily picks that's the session close, for weekly the close on the last day of the week, and for monthly the close on the last day of the month. While a window is still open, it shows the latest close so far.
  • Actual return: the closing price compared with the reference entry.
  • Target met?: ✅ Yes if the price touched the target at any point in the window, ❌ No if it didn't. The ± figure shows how far the closing price ended from the target.
  • Open a row to see the stop-loss and what happened if you followed the plan exactly (sell at the target, the stop or the end, whichever came first). Each batch is also compared with its market index (S&P 500 or Nifty 50) over the same window.

Your history is kept permanently

Every batch ever published, daily, weekly and monthly, stays on the site with all its details: reasons, key numbers, risks, sources, the market backdrop at the time, and the final result. Use Showing picks from on the Picks tab to open any past date, or browse Past picks & results, where batches are grouped by month. Batches that finished more than 45 days ago move into a permanent monthly archive (data/archive/) that loads when you open it, so the page stays fast however long the history grows. Each batch also has its own link you can bookmark. Nothing is ever deleted, and the full change history is also in the GitHub repo.

Keeping it fresh

A GitHub Action runs scripts/update_prices.py after the NSE and NYSE close each weekday. It updates prices and scores every past pick. To add new picks, put a new batch (with "horizon": "daily", "weekly" or "monthly") at the top of data/picks.js.

Honest limitations

  • Research-date prices come from news and quote pages, not an exchange feed. A few references are from a day or two earlier, and each card notes when.
  • Over a week or a month, much of a stock's move is noise. A good plan improves the odds but doesn't guarantee a profit.
Sources are linked inside each pick. Price updates use Yahoo Finance data. For education and research only.
